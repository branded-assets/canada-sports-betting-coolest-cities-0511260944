What stands out beyond the top spots

These separate examples compare the original composite-index leaders within each size category. They do not change when a different ranking measure is selected above.

Big cities: Washington, DC and Denver have their own claims

New York City leads the big-city index, but other large markets stand out on individual measures. Washington, DC has 256 qualifying sporting events, more than any other city in the score-leading big-city group, while Denver has 74 breweries, the most in that same score-leading group. The figures offer two distinct ways to look beyond the composite score.

Medium cities: New Orleans and Madison shine on different measures

Minneapolis takes the medium-city crown, but New Orleans leads the score-leading medium-city group with 284 music events and 40 museums. Madison records 354 parks, the highest park count among those score-leading medium cities. Together, the figures illustrate how cultural activity and outdoor amenities can produce very different city stories.

Small cities: Boise and Regina show there is more than one way to stand out

Richmond, Virginia, ranks first among small cities, yet runner-up Boise lists 104 music events and 66 sporting events, compared with Richmond’s 74 and 56. Regina has 362 parks, the most among the score-leading small cities. Leading a single measure is not the same as topping the category’s combined score.

Study design and scoring

Methodology

The study analyzed 150 cities (100 US and 50 Canadian) across six weighted indicators to identify the coolest cities. The sample includes 50 big, 62 medium and 38 small cities, all scored using the same benchmark.

How the indicators are weighted

Each of the six indicators contributes to both the availability and geographic concentration scores. These scores are then combined to produce the final Coolest Cities Score out of 100.

Availability is weighted more heavily to prioritize how much a city offers, while concentration recognizes how densely its offerings are distributed.

Event coverage

Music and sporting events were sourced from Ticketmaster and SeatGeek for November 2026 through January 2027, with identifiable duplicate listings and ticket products that were not separate events excluded.

How the score is calculated

Each indicator’s total count and count per square mile were log-transformed and normalized against their respective highest transformed values across all 150 cities. The six weighted indicators produce availability and geographic concentration scores, combined at 75% and 25%, respectively, to calculate the final Coolest Cities Score out of 100.

Study scope

The index measures the availability and geographic concentration of events and amenities, rather than their quality or attendance. Data was collected between November 1 and 7, 2026, reflecting listings available at the time of collection.
